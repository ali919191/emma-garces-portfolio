## 4. Long-form biography — `content_sections` entry

**Slug:** `about-emma` · **Title:** About Emma

**Summary:**
> An international runway model with eighteen years in fashion — from children's presentations in Houston to New York Fashion Week, couture and bridal runways, and now an intentional expansion into Dubai.

**Body:**

> Emma Garces was four years old the first time someone suggested she model. Her mother was a stay-at-home mom, and the two of them spent their days exploring Houston — the Galleria, Neiman Marcus, Saks. A manager noticed how tall she was for her age and suggested she model the children's collection.
>
> That led to her first runway show. She remembers the brightness of the lights and how nervous she was before stepping out. Then she started walking, and the nerves went somewhere else. "Something about it felt completely natural," she says, "almost as if I had discovered a place where I was meant to be."
>
> She kept being approached — in malls, on the street — and worked through several agencies as she grew up. Around thirteen, modeling stopped being something that happened to her and became something she pursued. By fifteen she had set herself a goal that seemed unreasonable at the time: New York Fashion Week.
>
> She reached it. In September 2021 she walked Negris LeBrum's Spring/Summer 2022 collection in New York — look 28. It became the longest working relationship of her career: more than ten projects with the house between 2021 and 2024, across New York and Houston, on the runway and in editorial.
>
> The work since has moved across registers. Couture for Luisa and Anna Gupta. Bridal for Designs by Sippy and Yumi Katsura. A Moroccan caftan for Hiba Couture. Automotive editorial with a 1963 Corvette outside Brasserie 19. The Larita runway in April 2025, The Grid Show at Houston Fashion Week that June, and the Poshak runway in August 2026 — her most recent show.
>
> Her international experience began before Dubai entered the picture: Paris Fashion Week and a château editorial in France in 2019, while she was still in her teens, plus virtual participation in Milan. Dubai is the next chapter, not the first one.
>
> Alongside the career, she is completing a Bachelor of Science in Cybersecurity and Information Assurance and works in AI operations at HARFT AI. She paints. She writes. She keeps a model bag packed the night before every call.

## 7. Professional approach — `content_sections` entry

Emma's own words, lightly trimmed. This is the strongest writing in the entire interview and should run close to verbatim.

> "Arriving fifteen minutes early means arriving on time."
>
> She prepares the night before — a model bag with several colours and heights of heel, nude and black foundations, strapless support, covers, wipes, water, snacks. A designer may add a second or third look at the last minute; if she does not have the right foundation or shoe, the garment cannot go out.
>
> In rehearsal she stays quiet and listens, because the stage director may not repeat the instruction. If someone else becomes confused, she holds the original direction rather than copying the mistake.
>
> "I treat every garment as a piece of art entrusted to me." Once dressed, she does not eat, does not sit unnecessarily, and takes care of anything she needs beforehand. If there is a problem with a garment she tells the dresser immediately rather than trying to fix it herself.
>
> She treats every designer, photographer, dresser, stylist and technician the same regardless of how established they are. "No number of shows or Fashion Weeks makes one person more important than another."
