## ⚠️ READ THIS FIRST — five findings that change what we can publish

**1. The interview document is already AI-processed, and it flags its own uncertainty.**
Several answers open with phrases like *"Here is a polished version that reconciles…"*. The document repeatedly instructs us to verify before publishing, and states plainly: *"I did not find enough Instagram evidence to describe Gucci, Chanel, Tom Ford, Versace, Kenneth Barlis, Un Deux Trois, or Miss Robinson as confirmed official designer engagements."* **Those seven names must not appear as credits.** Some prose in the document is a writing assistant's language, not Emma's — I have separated the two below.

**2. Identity is not verified in three folders.** Folder names are not proof that Emma is the model in the photograph. In `Hiba Couture`, the gold-gown images (`HIB01`, `HIB02`) appear to show a **different model** from the silver-caftan image (`HIB04`) — and the interview says Emma wore *"a regal silver Moroccan caftan."* The `Poshak` and `Indian Bride` folders may contain more than one model. **Emma must confirm identity image-by-image before any of these publish.**

Publishing watermarked images on an international portfolio reads as unprofessional and may be a licensing problem. **Clean files must be obtained from the photographer before use.**

**4. The strongest runway evidence is also the lowest resolution.** The Negris LeBrum × Launchmetrics NYFW set (`N21`) is the best professional material in the archive — and most of it is 950×1425 (Instagram-export size). It cannot carry a full-bleed desktop hero. Original files should be requested from the photographer/brand.

**5. Sensitive personal disclosures need explicit consent.** The interview contains a named mental-health disclosure, a detailed bullying account, religious identity, and an engagement. None of it should be published by default. See §K.
